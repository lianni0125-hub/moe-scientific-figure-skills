# Scientific Figure Principles

## Start from the claim

Write one sentence stating what the reader should learn from the figure. Build the visual hierarchy around that sentence. Remove elements that do not support it.

## Semantic colour registry

Before drawing, list every recurring construct and assign its visual role. Reuse the mapping throughout the paper. If two entities share a hue, readers should be able to infer that they belong to the same scientific category.

Colour must not be the sole carrier of an important distinction. Reinforce it with position, shape, line style, direct labels, or icons.

## Typography and geometry

- Test at final insertion size rather than on a zoomed canvas.
- Use short labels and direct annotation.
- Keep arrows visible, with breathing room at both ends.
- Use consistent box padding and alignment.
- Leave enough negative space to separate conceptual groups, but avoid decorative emptiness.
- Make the main takeaway and section-level labels visibly larger than annotations.

## Final check

- Are identical colours semantically identical?
- Are different scientific roles visually distinguishable?
- Is every label readable at final size?
- Do arrows touch neither text nor box borders?
- Does the figure remain understandable in greyscale?
- Are legends necessary, or would direct labels be clearer?
- Are panel letters and figure numbers handled by the manuscript rather than baked into the art?
